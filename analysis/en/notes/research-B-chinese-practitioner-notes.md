## 1. Pitfall: all-correct/all-wrong samples → intra-group advantage is identically zero

**【Phenomenon】** For one prompt sampling n items, when all are correct or all are wrong, the intra-group std = 0, `A_i=(r_i-mean)/std` is identically zero, the gradient is 0, and compute is wasted. A common description in the Chinese community: “When training reaches 200~500 steps, reward suddenly drops from 0.7 to 0 and can never recover” ([Zuo Yang: GRPO advanced algorithms](https://www.cnblogs.com/zuoyang/p/20669796), C, Cnblogs blogger, gives a “symptom/cause/fix” three-part structure).

**【Community explanation】**
- **Definitive level**: The DAPO paper uses this as its core motivation to propose **dynamic sampling**—oversample and filter out groups with accuracy 0 or 1 until the batch is filled with “gradient-bearing” samples. Highly upvoted Zhihu answers (answerers “If I Were Given an AI” and “LazyCat”) both list it as one of DAPO’s two core elements ([Zhihu: DAPO is all existing small tricks, why is it so popular](https://www.zhihu.com/question/1895273986537014226), B/C).
- **Review level**: A Chinese review covering 56 works points out that intra-group normalization simultaneously sows the hidden risk of zero advantage and **difficulty bias** (questions of different difficulty have different std, and their gradient contributions are uneven), and identifies **two unresolved debates in the field**: (a) four works use four proofs to show that “medium difficulty (success rate p≈0.5) is optimal”, but this is challenged by the counterexample under from-scratch RL that “difficult samples should be upweighted”; (b) **“discarding all-wrong difficult samples” (DAPO/GRESO) directly conflicts with “retaining or even upweighting difficult samples” (ReLIFT/LUFFY), and no one gives a criterion** ([RL difficult-sample learning review](https://blog.csdn.net/weixin_57133810/article/details/162177963), C).
- **An engineering detail**: If the implementation directly does `(r-mean)/std` without adding eps, when the group is entirely identical it will produce **NaN and make the loss explode**, not merely the mild “gradient is 0” ([CSDN, suspected AI-generated](https://blog.csdn.net/weixin_29057163/article/details/164523806), C, only as a clue]).

**【Handling methods】**
1. **Filtering/dynamic sampling** (DAPO route). **Official implementation detail**: the slime documentation states that filter function `check_reward_nonzero_std` “will check whether the reward standard deviation of a group of samples is greater than zero… to avoid data being too homogeneous”, and returns marker `zero_std_{...}`; and “if the filter function is very strict, causing a large number of prompt groups to be discarded, the system will monitor the number of pending tasks in `remaining_batch_size`. Once the number of pending tasks drops below the target number (32) because too many were discarded, the system will automatically trigger a new round of oversampling” ([slime official Chinese documentation](https://thudm.github.io/slime/zh/get_started/customization.html), A).
2. **Increase group size**: `num_generations` 8→16. The official verl best practices also give typical values: “GRPO is 64, DAPO is 16” ([verl best practices](https://verl.org.cn/en/latest/perf/best_practices.html), A).
3. **Increase the proportion of positive samples in the loss** (B, first-hand experience): An Alibaba RL engineer wrote: "If the model's success rate on a certain type of task is not high, you cannot directly use GRPO; you need to find a way to increase the proportion of positive samples in the loss… otherwise negative samples dominate and it easily collapses." The reason is that "traditional RL has a small action space; after suppressing erroneous Actions, the probabilities will naturally shift to correct Actions; but for LLM RL, the action space is vocabulary size multiplied by sequence length. After suppressing the probability of a certain sequence output, where these probabilities are allocated is unknown and chaotic" ([Using RL for LLM post-training: pitfalls and lessons from half a year](https://blog.csdn.net/weixin_40920183/article/details/157656504), B, the main text notes "Author: Tianqing@Zhihu (Alibaba employee)").
4. **Virtual full-score sample (NGRPO)**: Append a virtual sample with 1.0 to rewards to artificially create non-zero variance. One author measured "average score +13 points, tool accuracy +14 points" ([GRPO training pitfall log 63%→96%](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2005390937266857291), C, **personal practice, not a paper conclusion**).
5. **Scale differences (Zhihu, C)**: An answer under this question on Zhihu points out that for small models (0.5B/1B, some 7-8B), "R1 training rewards are sparse…… in one training sampling run, the effective reward values are very sparse, so training is very unstable," whereas for models above 100B, "rewards become dense" is uncommon; another answerer (Tsesea) argues from first principles that after removing the Critic, GRPO is "trading time for space," and "with a sufficiently large Batch, the variance of Policy Gradient can be effectively reduced," therefore **for small batch and small-to-medium-scale training, it is not recommended to use GRPO without a Critic** ([Zhihu: Why does GRPO easily blow up during training, and halfway through training the reward easily suddenly drops?](https://www.zhihu.com/question/1893241692582285916), C, the answerers are individual technical authors, not official conclusions]).

## 2. Pitfall: entropy collapse and the reverse entropy explosion

**【Phenomenon】** `actor/entropy` rapidly drops to near 0 in the early stage of training, outputs become homogenized, sampling paths narrow, and pass@1 stalls.

**【Explanation】**
- **Category A (included in official documentation)**: The official verl "entropy mechanism" page defines the "entropy collapse problem, i.e., policy entropy sharply decreases during training, leading to overconfidence and performance saturation," gives the empirical relationship `R = −a·exp(H) + b`, and mitigation methods Clip-Cov / KL-Cov, and states that "when the baseline's entropy reaches a plateau and can no longer be consumed, the KL-Cov method can still maintain an entropy level **more than 10 times higher**" — **but the official documentation likewise does not give an absolute entropy threshold** ([verl official Chinese documentation · entropy mechanism](https://verl.org.cn/en/latest/algo/entropy.html), A).
- **Mechanistic derivation supported by papers (C)**: Single-step entropy change `ΔH ≈ −η·Cov[log π(a|s), A(s,a)]`. In practice this covariance is **structurally always positive** — after SFT the model by default outputs high-probability tokens, and the reward model tends to give high scores to high-probability outputs, so "each update step systematically compresses the distribution"; group observations show that simple prompts have large covariance and fast entropy decrease, while difficult prompts have small covariance or even negative covariance ([Entropy collapse and reward collapse](https://www.cnblogs.com/Big-Yellow/p/19662616), C, the author self-describes as "collected and organized," with arXiv:2505.22617 / 2503.14476 / 2506.01939).
- **Fine-grained correction from an ACL Outstanding Paper (A/B)**: It explains using "reasoning tree branch growth/pruning" — branch growth (raising low-probability correct branches) increases entropy, pruning (suppressing low-probability incorrect branches) decreases entropy, and **healthy training requires a dynamic balance between the two**. This interpretation directly criticizes the coarseness of community methods: DAPO raising clip-high "changes the clipping threshold, rather than directly judging how much entropy change each token will bring in the current update"; and gives a counterintuitive conclusion — **"updating high-entropy tokens only means they may bring a larger entropy change; it does not guarantee the direction of change. If these tokens are currently in the entropy-decreasing direction, amplifying them may instead accelerate entropy collapse."** Its method STEER downweights tokens with drastic entropy changes (a token-level "speed limiter") ([ACL Outstanding Paper Chinese interpretation](https://www.163.com/dy/article/L1SMLG2I0511AQHO.html), A/B).
- **"Collapse is not necessarily low entropy" (C)**: The RAGEN series proposes multi-turn collapse / Echo Trap, pointing out that collapse in agent training **is not necessarily entropy becoming lower; it may also be input dependence becoming lower**, and the handling method is SNR-aware filtering (only keep prompts with high intra-group reward variance to participate in update) ([RAGEN-related Chinese interpretation](https://estrellajer.github.io/blog/2026/agentic-rl/), C, it is recommended to check back on arXiv before citing).

**【Solutions】** Clip-Higher (DAPO, settled conclusion): decouple upper and lower clipping into ε_low / ε_high; the paper uses **0.2 / 0.28**, giving low-probability tokens more room to rise; TRL implementation `GRPOConfig(epsilon_low=0.1, epsilon_high=0.3)`. There is also **dynamic temperature / computing gradients only on high-entropy tokens** (the 80/20 rule from arXiv:2506.01939, parameter `top_entropy_quantile`, TRL default 1.0, paper recommends **0.2**); the paper's counterintuitive experimental conclusion is that **discarding low-entropy tokens works better than using all tokens** (see above [Entropy collapse and reward collapse](https://www.cnblogs.com/Big-Yellow/p/19662616), C). **Note**: STEER's interpretation explicitly opposes "crudely pulling entropy up," because "excessive entropy increase may likewise cause training to diverge."

## 3. Pitfall: training-inference mismatch / MoE routing / TIS correction

**【Phenomenon】** For the same token sequence, the logprob computed by the inference engine (vLLM/SGLang) and the training engine (HF/Megatron) is inconsistent; **with Sync=1 (sample one batch, train one batch, data theoretically brand new), a large number of samples are still clipped by importance sampling, and the clip ratio rises with training time**.

**【Category A: judgment thresholds given by official documentation (the only "official-level" values in this survey)】**
- **Gold-standard metric**: `training/rollout_probs_diff_mean` "Under normal circumstances… it should be below **0.005**. If you observe this value above **0.01**, this indicates a precision problem in the inference engine." The title of this entry in the verl FAQ is exactly **"Inference and training sequence mismatch (actor/grad_norm too high)"** — that is, the official documentation also attributes "grad_norm too high" to training-inference mismatch. Known triggering conditions: non-Hopper architecture GPUs (A100/L20/B200), using vLLM with issue 22103, long input/output text; temporary solution `...vllm.disable_cascade_attn=True` ([verl FAQ](https://verl.org.cn/en/latest/faq/faq.html), A).
- **IS/TIS thresholds**: `rollout_is` (token) "**common thresholds 1.5–5.0**"; `rollout_is` (sequence) "**common thresholds 2.0–10.0**"; `rollout_is_threshold` **defaults to 2.0**, TIS is implemented via `.clamp(max=...)`. Troubleshooting criterion: "`rollout_is_std` > **1.0**, `rollout_is_eff_sample_size` < **0.3**」= 权重方差过大；「`rollout_is_mean` < **0.5** 或 > **2.0**" = the mean deviates too far from 1.0 ([verl · Rollout correction](https://verl.org.cn/en/latest/algo/rollout_corr.html), A]).
- **AReaL's criterion**: "**importance weight metric…** the average should stay close to 1.0**"; if it deviates, decrease `ppo_n_minibatches`; if `n_minibatches==1` still deviates (**common in MoE training**) then increase `actor.megatron.use_deterministic_algorithms=1`; `behav_imp_weight_cap` **recommended value 5** ([AReaL official Chinese documentation](https://github.com/inclusionAI/AReaL/blob/main/docs/zh/best_practices/algo_perf.md), A]).
- **OpenRLHF**: `--algo.advantage.is_correction_threshold 0.5 5.0` (the [low, high] interval of the gating statistic), correction modes are divided into "**mask (ICEPOP / seq-mask-tis) | clip (TIS, token-level only)**" ([OpenRLHF Chinese README](https://github.com/OpenRLHF/OpenRLHF/blob/main/README_zh.md), A]).
- **verl's official statement on the crash mechanism**: the DPPO page points out that "methods without a trust region (PG-IS, CISPO) or methods with an incorrectly specified trust region (MiniRL) will face **increasingly severe mismatch and eventually crash**"; and criticizes PPO clipping for "**over-penalizing low-probability tokens**… while **under-penalizing high-probability tokens**" ([verl · DPPO](https://verl.org.cn/en/latest/algo/dppo.html), A]).

**[B/C category supplement]**
- **First-hand experience from an Alibaba employee**: vLLM/SGLang "the predicted sequence logprob and the one inferred by Huggingface are not completely equivalent"; "one potential idea is to not fully trust vLLM's logprob and recompute the Prefill phase with HF"; the empirical conclusion for sequence-level loss is "**GSPO converges slower but more stably for Dense models, and GSPO has optimizations for MoE, so for MoE models just use GSPO without thinking**", "in other cases GRPO, DAPO, Reinforce++ are not very different" ([half a year of pitfalls](https://blog.csdn.net/weixin_40920183/article/details/157656504), B]).
- **MoE routing (C, Zhihu column)**: expert routing is a random variable and enters both sampling and forward pass, "compared with Dense models, the variance of the gradient estimate increases significantly". Three routes: **R2** replays the routing of `π_old`, **R3** replays the routing of the sampling policy, and the author's preferred **multiple forward passes taking different expert-routing samples and averaging them**; he believes routing-replay **will limit the exploration space** ("the same (x,y) pair may correspond to multiple different expert-routings"), and further gives Multiple IS and GEPO (using the sequence-level group expectation as the IS denominator). This method has been verified on 235B MoE and Kuaishou KAT-Coder-Pro V1 ([initial exploration of MOE-RL training-inference consistency](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20260102A01TVI00), C, Tencent News reposted from a Zhihu column, author haotian]).
- **Vendor statement**: Tencent Hunyuan 2.0 release materials say "**importance sampling correction** alleviated the training-inference inconsistency problem" ([Tencent Cloud Developer Community](https://cloud.tencent.com/developer/article/2598835), B, body text scraping was blocked, cited only as a statement]).
- **A highly controversial conclusion (not endorsed)**: a CSDN article claims that BF16 has a 7-bit mantissa and FP16 has 10 bits, saying that a certain model "crashes at step 0" under BF16 and that all problems disappear after switching to FP16. **This contradicts mainstream practice**; the original paper was not rechecked ([CSDN](https://blog.csdn.net/weixin_36378508/article/details/155710405), C, highly controversial]).

## 4. Pitfall: KL anomalies, off-policy, and staleness

**[Phenomenon]** `actor/ppo_kl` spikes or remains 0 for a long time; under async, the older the data, the harder it is to train; an Alibaba employee's exact words: "RL cannot scale like pretraining and SFT; it may crash after a few thousand steps, and metrics such as entropy, KL, reward, PPO loss, and output length suddenly become abnormal".

**[Explanation and handling]**
- **Removing KL (DAPO, settled)**: when training long-CoT reasoning models, the policy distribution deviates significantly from the initial model, so a KL constraint is unnecessary.
- **Moving KL into the reward (DeepSeek-V3.2 statement, paraphrased in C)**: move KL from a separate reward term into the reward function to correct estimation bias; and introduce a binary mask `M_i,t` to mask out "negative-advantage sequences that introduce significant policy deviation" ([5 major problems of GRPO collapse](https://yunpan.plus/t/6550-1-1), C, the article itself states "focuses on engineering experience… not a strict theoretical conclusion"]).
- **Category A: slime official Debug guide's KL troubleshooting mnemonic**: first check "`log_probs` and `ref_log_probs` **are exactly equal (i.e., kl=0 at the first step), and the value is small**"; "if the value is relatively large (e.g. **>1**), there are generally 2 possibilities… if the value is very large, the training configuration is probably wrong; if the value is only slightly larger than the state of sft loss, for example an instruct model's logprob reaches 0.8, it may be that the data does not conform to the training chat template"; for one-inference-one-training (`num_steps_per_rollout == 1`), "whether kl is 0 and whether grad_norm is small". The same page also records a **silent failure**: misconfiguring the ignore list of an INT4/Compressed-Tensors quantized checkpoint will make "MoE routing weights (`mlp.gate.weight`) all zeros" ([slime · Debug guide](https://thudm.github.io/slime/zh/developer_guide/debug.html), A]).
- **Category A: verl's quantitative recommendation for async**: "`staleness_threshold`… **it is recommended to set this value to less than 1**"; and acknowledges that "in the later stage of training, **metrics and generation length may become unstable**"; for `bypass_mode=False` + RIS, "our implementation approximates AReaL's Decoupled PPO" ([verl · fully asynchronous policy trainer](https://verl.org.cn/en/latest/advance/fully_async.html), A]).
- **Category B counterintuitive experience**: "regarding the Sync value, in fact a larger Sync is not necessarily worse; I have seen scenarios where **Sync=10 works better than Sync=1**. But fully asynchronous training still requires caution, and it is best to use a **Priority Buffer** to put relatively new data in an earlier position"; and gives a sense of magnitude: "with Sync=1… if machine utilization can reach **50%**, that is already good" ([half a year of pitfalls](https://blog.csdn.net/weixin_40920183/article/details/157656504), B]).

## 5. Pitfall: reward hacking / verifier cheating (corresponding to the penalty, harness, env metric family)

**[Phenomenon (B, exact words from the ROLL team)]**
- **Test file leakage**: "Although test files are isolated by directory and access control, the model may still access them indirectly through certain paths or commands... the model will very quickly 'slack off': instead of reasoning carefully about the task, it directly reads or even modifies the test script"; during training, "the number of test script invocations rises significantly, and ultimately a large number of rollouts degenerate into directly executing the test file".
- **False positive data**: In early synthetic data, the false positive ratio was once as high as **about 40%**. Example: a task requires setting up git→push→deploy to 8080, but the test script only `curl localhost:8080` checks the return; the Agent can pass by directly writing `hello.html` to the web root directory.
- **Other cheating modes**: modifying the established environment, tool overuse (brute-force retries), search abuse, destructive operations (deleting files/killing processes), covert shortcuts.
- **length hacking**: Research from Jiuzhang Zhisuan Cloud and Renmin University's STILL project shows that "simply rewarding longer answers easily produces length hacking" ([Jiuzhang Zhisuan Cloud "training-inference consistency" ](https://tech.ifeng.com/c/8vh2fw9JX1Q), B).

**【Handling methods】**
- **Environment cleanup + strict isolation of test files**: The ROLL team "actively cleans up intermediate files generated during environment initialization or Agent installation before rollout" and "test files are **uploaded only during the final evaluation stage**".
- **Category A (slime official approach)**: Let the agent modify code in an **independent sandbox** to produce `git diff`, then take the diff to a **second clean sandbox** to grade against the test harness (the original source marks it "no test-cheating") ([slime · Coding-Agent RL example ](https://thudm.github.io/slime/zh/_examples_synced/coding_agent_rl/README.html), A).
- **Two gates before data ingestion**: **Ground-truth validation** (golden solution cannot pass all tests → discard) + **No-op validation** (doing nothing also passes the tests → discard), plus LLM-as-judge multi-model collaborative review of "instruction–test" pairs.
- **Fine-grained behavior monitoring (signal checklist listed by the ROLL team)**: success rate trends for different tasks; **success/failure rates of different tools**; repeated or looping tool call patterns; usage frequency of different tools; **usage frequency of different commands**. "Once similar patterns are detected, we will roll back training, or locate and remove the instances causing the problem." It also emphasizes sandbox concurrency monitoring—"we encountered training anomalies caused by environment concurrency jitter at multiple stages, and these problems would be very difficult to locate without system-level service observability".
- **Reward capped at 1.0 + targeted penalty (C)**: The above 7B SubAgent transcript found that the sum of reward components maxed out at only 0.8, "all correct outputs scored the same, with no discrimination"; after changing it to 1.0 it "immediately improved by 6%"; then, targeting the model's overuse of `check_market_status`, a −0.3 targeted penalty was added, concluding that "designing penalties for specific error patterns is more effective than general algorithm improvements".
- **A Chinese anti-cheating checklist (C, thresholds are not extrapolatable)**: reward by length→meaningless padding; by number of tool calls→frantic calling; looking only at the final answer→garbling in think to make up an answer; sole LLM scoring→pleasing the Judge. Implementation layer: **token > 1000 deduct 30%, `tool_calls` > 8 then decay**; recommends **manually reviewing 20 high-reward + 20 low-reward trajectories sampled every 100 steps** ([Agentic RL Chinese tutorial ](https://haozhe-xing.github.io/agent_learning/zh/chapter_agentic_rl/05_grpo.html), C).
- A study by HKUST was summarized by Chinese media as "model verifiers are easily hacked" ([Tencent News ](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20250602A04CQD00), B).

Synthesized sources above: [The Bitter Lesson! ROLL team shares: practical experience in Agentic RL training ](https://blog.csdn.net/QingKeLab/article/details/158100042) (B, reposted by CSDN; also a [pitfalls version ](https://blog.csdn.net/qq_35812205/article/details/158209552)).

## 6. Pitfall: long-tail rollout, GPU idling, partial rollout, and asynchrony

**【Phenomenon】** The end time of a batch depends on the longest trajectory, "after many short trajectories finish, the GPU still needs to idle and wait"; some sources say "**long-tail requests in rollout may slow down 90% of training time**" ([Guancha Fengwen ](https://user.guancha.cn/main/content?id=1569838), C). The official statement on throughput imbalance: "If the Trainer can consume 100 samples per second, while the Generator can only generate 60, training compute will sit idle; conversely, it will cause Rollout accumulation, data staleness, forming **Policy Staleness**" ([Jiuzhang Zhisuan Cloud ](https://tech.ifeng.com/c/8vh2fw9JX1Q), B).

**【Four frameworks, four long-tail strategies (B/C)】** Source: [Agentic RL framework side-by-side comparison ](https://yunpan.plus/t/5916-1-1)

| Framework | Strategy | Key mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| AReaL (Ant) | Fully asynchronous Stream Rollout | **Decoupled PPO with two-level importance sampling** corrects gradient bias from stale data; **Interruptible Generation**: when data is insufficient, suspend long tasks and prioritize producing short tasks to stabilize batch size |
| Seer (Moonshot AI) | Strict on-policy + system-level load balancing | **Divided Rollout** (chunk long requests to fill bubbles), **Global KV Cache** (avoids repeated Prefill), **Context-Aware Scheduling** (prioritize long tasks), AGSD adaptive grouped speculative decoding |
| verl (ByteDance) | Configurable synchronous/partially asynchronous/fully asynchronous | **`staleness_threshold`** controls the proportion of old data; **Partial Rollout / Sleep-Resume** |
| Slime (Zhipu) | Hybrid mode + active partial sampling | **APR oversubscription**: if you want batch 32, send 64 requests simultaneously; when the fastest 32 finish, terminate the rest but retain KV Cache; the **TITO gateway** re-encodes text with the training cluster's tokenizer; for offline data, use **two-sided importance sampling** |

- **Slime's TITO gateway** solves the core challenge under asynchrony of "mismatched model versions and tokenizers between the generation side and the training side" ([GLM-5 Slime asynchronous RL framework explained ](https://yunpan.plus/t/14782-1-1), C).
- **verl's official Agentic RL documentation** requires 「avoid keeping the GPU idle while waiting for tool call results」, using asyncio async + load balancing to 「reduce the impact of long-tail requests on performance」 ([verl · Agentic RL ](https://verl.org.cn/en/latest/start/agentic_rl.html), A).
- **Type A cost explanation (slime)**: the benefit of fully-async is "the next training step doesn't wait for the slowest in-flight sample"; **the cost is that ABORTED trajectories must be rerun in full, with no partial resume** ([slime · Fully-Async Rollout](https://thudm.github.io/slime/zh/_examples_synced/fully_async/README.html), A).
- **Type C concrete implementation cost (with PR provenance)**: verl's partial rollout has two schemes—`max_age` segmented inference, `StreamScheduler` + async vLLM; the cost is **old_logp bias on long-tail samples, low training frequency for long sequences causing biased results, and GRPO requiring whole-group requeue** ([CSDN](https://blog.csdn.net/h_2025/article/details/156594148), C, the article gives verl PR 1826 / 2200).
- **Type C**: GLM-5's analysis mentions that CUDA nondeterminism in MoE routing causes training instability, and it eventually switched to a forced-deterministic implementation, concluding that "**engineering details (asynchronous scheduling, CUDA determinism, environment stability) often determine success or failure more than the algorithm itself**" ([GLM-5 Agentic RL engineering analysis](https://xavierzhang2002.github.io/agentic-rl-analysis/agentic-rl/ch1/1.4-engineering/), C).

**【Type B: the ROLL team's "training instability troubleshooting checklist" (the most practical one)】** Original text: "When training becomes unstable, we usually check the following signals first: ① Are a small number of extreme trajectories dominating the update? (Typical features: abnormally long failed trajectories, accompanied by heavy-tailed negative rewards) → use masking, lower the weight, tighten clipping; ② Are negative samples dominating overall? → lower the weight of negative samples, filter low-confidence failed samples, or use curriculum-style training; ③ Is the model learning 'bad patterns'? → introduce behavior penalties, higher-dimensional reward design." Two empirical principles: **"prioritize targeted handling of extreme trajectories… if it is still unstable, then adopt global reweighting"**; **"RL gradients are usually noisier than supervised learning, so a smaller learning rate, together with stronger constraints, annealing, or adaptive mechanisms, is often more stable."**

## 7. Differences between Agentic RL and single-turn RL

**【Essential difference (B, ROLL team)】** "RLVR trains a model that 'can answer,' while Agentic RL trains a model that 'can act'"; RLVR is "more like an in-context bandit problem," while Agentic RL is a "multi-step interactive MDP."

**【Type A: hard requirements in official documentation (definitive layer)】**
- **Multi-turn must be token-in / token-out (verl official)**: "Conversion between tokens and text may be irreversible…… you must strictly use the tokens generated by LLM inference to avoid inaccurate advantage computation" ([verl · Agentic RL](https://verl.org.cn/en/latest/start/agentic_rl.html), A).
- **Official handling rules for prefix drift (slime)**: if the prefix tokens do not match the sampled output, **discard the unmatched suffix**; if the drift cuts in the middle of the previous model output segment, **keep that segment but set `loss_mask` all to 0**. The original text highlights the risk: "A re-tokenization mismatch can make a string-level conversation look continuous while token-level provenance is broken." ([slime · Coding-Agent RL](https://thudm.github.io/slime/zh/_examples_synced/coding_agent_rl/README.html), A).
- **Treat sandbox noise as a first-class citizen**: `SWE_BOOT_CONCURRENCY=16` (mitigate startup long tail), `SWE_AGENT_TIME_BUDGET_SEC=1800`, `SWE_EVAL_TIMEOUT_SEC=600`, `SWE_ROLLOUT_GUARD_SEC = agent+eval+180` (outer fallback). Same page (A).
- **verl's self-built sandbox service default rate limiting and its admitted shortcomings**: `rate_limit=10` concurrency, `default_timeout=30s` (prevent 429); the documentation itself states, "**does not consider observability metrics. Does not involve distributed failover and component fault tolerance**" ([verl · Sandbox Fusion](https://verl.org.cn/en/latest/sglang_multiturn/sandbox_fusion.html), A).
- **A "false anomaly" reminder (verl official)**: the console showing `Failed to decode tool call` "**does not mean training is abnormal**" (A). **slime official** also notes: garbled text is usually "megatron was not loaded correctly" (check whether `--load`/`--ref-load` has a ckpt containing `latest_checkpointed_iteration.txt`); for "grad norm is very high, training has collapsed," first check "whether the chat template is consistent with the original model"; for NaN/Inf gradients, `--no-check-for-nan-in-loss-and-grad` can be used to skip that step ([slime · Common Q&A](https://thudm.github.io/slime/zh/get_started/qa.html), A).

**【Type B/C difference points】**
- **Different optimization unit**: ROLL proposes computing returns and importance sampling (IPA) at the **interaction chunk** level (the segment from one environment interaction to the next, usually ending with a tool call) rather than at the token level; "when the deviation between the inference policy and the training policy is too large, **mask the entire chunk**, rather than masking token by token."
- **Re-tokenization shift (C)**: "If the token sequence output by the model changes during re-encoding, it will introduce a huge Off-policy bias"; **indiscriminate penalty on locally correct actions (C)**: in early multi-turn stages, correct and incorrect trajectories have highly overlapping embeddings, and "huge negative gradients from incorrect trajectories may 'hurt' correct trajectories"; one solution is similar to changing the advantage value from (+1,−1) to (+1,0) ([GRPO collapse 5 major problems](https://yunpan.plus/t/6550-1-1), C).
- **The "concatenated multi-turn" trap of Thinking models (B, frontline experience, very critical)**: An Alibaba employee wrote—"Their multi-turn dialogue is not general multi-turn dialogue, but concatenated multi-turn dialogue…… Take Qwen3 as an example: at Turn 2, it deletes the thinking part from Turn 1… in this way, standard multi-turn GRPO becomes unusable"; the conclusion is that "all papers and work aimed at GRPO need to be reconsidered under this paradigm." Kimi's multiple tool call scenario is more complex. The same article also suggests for small models in multi-turn, "it is best to repeat the original Target and the previous few rounds of Action in the Prompt to tell the model at each turn."
- **Early use of only positive-sample trajectories (B, ROLL team)**: "When data is not yet fully reliable, training with only positive-sample trajectories is noticeably more stable"; "On large-scale synthetic data, updating with both positive and negative trajectories often collapses frequently". Curriculum-style strategy: early positive-only, later after data is expert-validated, then introduce negative trajectories. The author specifically clarifies that this **is not equivalent to RFT** (the loss is still the standard RL objective, retaining masking/clipping/normalization).
- **Environment augmentation (B, ROLL team)**: intentionally introduce different software versions, different telemetry sources, and even "remove a preinstalled dependency or switch to an unavailable telemetry source", forcing the Agent to "learn to check, diagnose, and recover".
- **Failure modes are "dirtier" (B, ROLL team)**: heavy-tailed distribution and extreme negative rewards; **shallow policy patterns** (repeated trial-and-error / fixed command sequences reinforced by outcome reward); **noise-type failures** (caused by environment randomness, with lower confidence for negative samples than positive samples).
- **A failure timeline usable as a template (B, ROLL team)**: training score drops sharply at step ~80; on review, it was found that **starting from step ~50, the maximum response length of failed trajectories rises rapidly, while the number of failed samples remains basically unchanged**——"mainly the impact of a small number of extreme failed trajectories". After masking out failed trajectories longer than **20k**, the advantage mean recovers; after about 40 steps it becomes unstable again, and the earliest signal is "**the number of negative samples gradually increases**", so negative samples are globally reweighted.
- **No universal solution (B)**: "Under different data conditions, the same strategy may produce opposite effects…… In one case, removing the standard deviation (std) quickly causes training to collapse; in another case, the same operation instead makes training more stable."

## 10. Three conclusions for "dashboard readers"

1. **Look at zero advantage first, then entropy**: `dynsam/passrate/zero`, `passrate/one`, `hist9_ratio` determine whether this step has a learning signal.
2. **If `pg_clipfrac` / `ppo_kl` / `pg_tis_clipfrac` are abnormal, suspect training-inference mismatch first, then learning rate**: the official criterion is `training/rollout_probs_diff_mean` (<0.005 正常，>0.01 means a precision issue).
3. **Don't expect a unified threshold**: officially, only several types of thresholds are given—IS weight, importance weight, and truncation ratio; what the Chinese community can provide is a **troubleshooting order** (extreme trajectories → negative sample proportion → bad patterns → global reweighting → lower learning rate).
